# Where liver cancer treatment truly succeeds

As of November 2026, the clearest successes in primary liver cancer sit at the two ends of the disease. At one end are early-stage, transplantable or resectable tumours: within-Milan hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC) transplant gives **75% four-year survival** in the founding series and **81% five-year survival** in modern UNOS-era controls. Hepatoblastoma in children is now mostly curable, with **3-year overall survival of 95%** for standard risk and **83% for high risk**. At the advanced end, the gains are smaller but real. In HCC, immunotherapy doublets have roughly doubled long-term survival compared with sorafenib: HIMALAYA's STRIDE regimen leaves **19.6% vs 9.4% of patients alive at 5 years**, and atezolizumab plus bevacizumab, nivolumab plus ipilimumab and lenvatinib/sorafenib are the other approved first-line options. In intrahepatic cholangiocarcinoma (iCCA), adding durvalumab to gemcitabine–cisplatin raises **3-year survival from 6.9% to 14.6% (HR 0.74)**, and molecular profiling opens targeted options for the roughly 1 in 10 patients with an FGFR2 fusion and the larger IDH1-mutant group. The notable failures matter just as much for an honest app. Adjuvant atezolizumab–bevacizumab (IMbrave050), TACE-plus-systemic combinations (EMERALD-1, LEAP-012), lenvatinib plus pembrolizumab (LEAP-002) and first-line pemigatinib (FIGHT-302) all improved recurrence-free or progression-free survival but **did not improve overall survival**. Nivolumab monotherapy and infigratinib lost their US approvals. For HepatoScope, the evidence supports a two-mode design. Patient mode uses plain-language, natural-frequency statements anchored to stage. Clinician mode uses provenance-stamped answer cards that separate approved, accelerated, withdrawn and investigational status. Several commonly quoted numbers remain unverified and are listed at the end of this report. They should not be displayed until checked.

## Four immunotherapy regimens beat sorafenib in advanced HCC, and three failures define the limits

HCC accounts for most primary liver cancer. Its stage-to-treatment logic is set by BCLC 2022. Expected median survival is **more than 5 years for BCLC 0/A, 2.5 years for B, 2 years for C and 3 months for D**. Atezolizumab–bevacizumab is the "first-choice first-line treatment," and tremelimumab plus durvalumab was added as a first-line option ([BCLC 2022](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8866082/)). One wording caution for HepatoScope: the fetched BCLC text supports "2.5 years" and "2 years" without a "greater than" sign. The ">" variants should appear only if someone has checked the guideline figure itself.

The median OS values of 23.7 months (CheckMate-9DW) and 23.8 months (CARES-310) look better than IMbrave150's 19.2 months. That comparison is invalid, because the control arms differ: CheckMate-9DW's control was mostly lenvatinib, and its control arm itself reached 20.6 months. The app should block cross-trial ranking.

### First-line and locoregional failures the app must show honestly

**LEAP-002** (lenvatinib + pembrolizumab vs lenvatinib) reached OS of 21.2 vs 19.0 months, HR 0.84. Its P value of .0227 missed the prespecified threshold of .0185, so the trial missed both co-primary endpoints despite an ORR of 26.1% vs 17.5% ([ASCO Post](https://ascopost.com/issues/november-10-2022-supplement-conference-highlights-esmo-2022/lenvatinib-plus-pembrolizumab-in-hepatocellular-carcinoma-leap-002-fails-to-meet-co-primary-endpoints/)).

Intermediate-stage combinations also stalled. **EMERALD-1** improved PFS (15.0 vs 8.2 months, HR 0.77), but its final OS analysis showed **no benefit**: 29.9 months with durvalumab + bevacizumab + TACE vs 33.3 months with TACE alone (HR 1.10, p=0.47). Grade 3/4 adverse events nearly doubled (47.4% vs 25.0%) ([Oncology News Australia](https://oncologynews.com.au/?p=61030)). **LEAP-012** improved PFS (14.6 vs 10.0 months, HR 0.66), but OS was not significant at the prespecified interim analysis and the study is being closed ([CancerNetwork](https://www.cancernetwork.com/view/pembrolizumab-combo-does-not-significantly-improve-os-in-unresectable-hcc)). **EMERALD-3** reported a PFS HR of 0.70 for STRIDE + lenvatinib + TACE, with OS still immature ([Oncology News Australia](https://oncologynews.com.au/?p=61030)). As of November 2026, no TACE-plus-systemic combination has proven an overall-survival benefit, and HepatoScope should never label PFS gains as "living longer."

### Curative therapy succeeds, and adjuvant therapy so far does not

Mazzaferro's 1996 NEJM series defined the Milan criteria: a single tumour of 5 cm or less, or up to 3 tumours each 3 cm or less, with no vascular invasion or spread. In 48 transplanted patients, **4-year survival was 75% and recurrence-free survival 83%**. The 35 patients who truly met the criteria on pathology did even better (85% vs 50% survival) ([Wiki Journal Club](https://www.wikijournalclub.org/wiki/Liver_Transplantation_for_the_Treatment_of_Small_Hepatocellular_Carcinomas_in_Patients_with_Cirrhosis)).

Prevention is a success story too. In 22,500 US veterans, curing hepatitis C with direct-acting antivirals cut HCC incidence to 0.90 vs 3.45 per 100 person-years (**adjusted HR 0.28, 95% CI 0.22–0.36**) ([MDedge](https://mdedge.com/content/liver-cancer-risk-lower-after-sustained-response-daas)).

After resection or ablation, adjuvant therapy is still a failure. IMbrave050 initially improved recurrence-free survival. With 35.1 months of follow-up, the RFS HR fell to **0.90** and median RFS was 33.2 vs 36.0 months. OS was immature but numerically worse (HR 1.26; 54 vs 46 deaths, with 30% crossover). The authors, publishing in J Hepatol in June 2026, concluded that the benefit–risk balance does not support routine use ([OncoDaily](https://oncodaily.com/oncolibrary/gastrointestinal-oncology/imbrave050-hcc)). Modern 5-year survival figures for resection, ablation and contemporary transplant cohorts were **not verified** and should not be shown as fact. The verified modern transplant figure (81%) appears in the real-world section below.

## Chemo-immunotherapy and genomic targeting define success in intrahepatic cholangiocarcinoma

iCCA is usually reported inside the broader biliary tract cancer (BTC) trials. Its first-line backbone, gemcitabine plus cisplatin, came from **ABC-02**: OS 11.7 vs 8.1 months, HR 0.64 (0.52–0.80), PFS 8.0 vs 5.0 months ([Hull repository, Valle NEJM 2010](https://hull-repository.worktribe.com/output/467879)). Two PD-1/PD-L1 additions improved on that backbone.

**TOPAZ-1** (durvalumab + GemCis; FDA approval **Sep 2, 2022**) showed a primary OS HR of 0.80 (0.66–0.97), with median OS 12.8 vs 11.5 months ([ASCO Post](https://ascopost.com/news/september-2022/fda-approves-durvalumab-based-combination-for-locally-advanced-or-metastatic-biliary-tract-cancer/)). At 41.3 months of follow-up the benefit widened to **HR 0.74 (0.63–0.87)**, with **3-year OS of 14.6% vs 6.9%** ([OncLive](https://onclive.com/view/durvalumab-plus-chemo-sustains-3-year-os-benefit-in-advanced-biliary-tract-cancer)). **KEYNOTE-966** (pembrolizumab + GemCis; FDA approval **Oct 31, 2023**) showed OS HR 0.83 (0.72–0.95) and median OS 12.7 vs 10.9 months ([FDA](https://www.fda.gov/drugs/resources-information-approved-drugs/fda-approves-pembrolizumab-chemotherapy-biliary-tract-cancer)), with 24-month OS of 25% vs 18% ([OncLive](https://www.onclive.com/view/pembrolizumab-plus-cisplatin-gemcitabine-extends-os-in-advanced-biliary-tract-cancer)). These trials were never compared head to head. A plain-language patient line that fits the TOPAZ-1 data is: "about 1 in 7 people alive at 3 years, compared with about 1 in 14 with chemotherapy alone."

Second-line chemotherapy gives modest benefit. In **ABC-06**, mFOLFOX plus active symptom control gave OS of 6.2 vs 5.3 months, HR 0.69 (0.50–0.97), with 6-month OS of 50.6% vs 35.5% ([NHS Medicines Resources](https://www.medicinesresources.nhs.uk/second-line-folfox-chemotherapy-versus-active-symptom-control-for-advanced-biliary-tract-cancer-abc-06-a-phase-3-open-label-randomised-controlled-trial-1.html)). The Korean phase 2b **NIFTY** trial (liposomal irinotecan + 5-FU/LV) reported PFS 7.1 vs 1.4 months (HR 0.56) and OS 8.6 vs 5.5 months (HR 0.68) in its primary analysis ([ASCO Post](https://ascopost.com/news/october-2021/addition-of-liposomal-irinotecan-to-fluorouracil-and-leucovorin-as-second-line-treatment-for-metastatic-biliary-tract-cancer/)). The final NIFTY analysis was not verified.

### Molecular targets turn a minority of iCCA into a different disease

ESMO recommends multigene NGS for iCCA, with RNA-based NGS for best fusion detection, and ranks FGFR2 fusions and IDH1 mutations as **ESCAT level I** ([ESMO factsheet](https://oncologypro.esmo.org/education-library/factsheets-on-biomarkers/fgfr2-fusions-testing-in-intrahepatic-cholangiocarcinoma)). One review gives these frequencies: FGFR2 fusions/rearrangements **8–13%** (9.4% rearrangements in 6,130 cases), IDH1 **roughly 14–29%**, BRAF V600E about 5%, HER2 amplification 1.6–4%, MSI-H about 1.2%, and NTRK and RET under 2% ([Hepatoma Research 2023](https://www.oaepublish.com/articles/2394-5079.2023.68)). That is a single source in a lower-tier journal, so HepatoScope should display these as ranges.

The most important 2026 development is a qualified result. **FIGHT-302** (first-line pemigatinib vs GemCis, ASCO 2026) improved PFS to 8.3 vs 6.8 months (HR 0.58, 0.39–0.87) and roughly doubled response duration (14.2 vs 6.3 months). It showed **no overall-survival benefit** (24.4 vs 25.0 months), probably because of crossover. As of May 2026, no first-line approval or filing had been reported ([OncLive](https://www.onclive.com/view/fight-302-data-to-detail-pemigatinib-s-phase-3-showing-vs-chemo-in-frontline-fgfr2-cca-at-asco-2026)), and first-line use "is investigational and has not been approved by the FDA" ([Cholangiocarcinoma Foundation](https://www.cholangiocarcinoma.org/phase-3-fight-302-results-presented-at-asco-2026-evaluate-pemigatinib-as-a-potential-first-line-treatment-approach-for-patients-with-fgfr2-fusion-or-rearrangement-positive-cholangiocarcinoma/)). Ivosidenib's survival claim depends on crossover adjustment in the same way, so clinician cards should show both the ITT HR (0.79, not significant) and the adjusted HR (0.49).

### Curative iCCA: resection, capecitabine and selective transplant

For resected disease, BILCAP's long-term analysis (median follow-up 106 months) is less clear-cut than early reports. The adjusted ITT OS was **49.6 vs 36.1 months, HR 0.84 (0.67–1.06)**, which is not significant. A sensitivity analysis gave HR 0.74 (0.59–0.94), and the per-protocol HR was 0.79 (52.3 vs 36.1 months). Five-year RFS was 34% vs 31% ([ASCO Post](https://ascopost.com/news/march-2022/long-term-survival-outcomes-with-adjuvant-capecitabine-in-biliary-tract-cancer/)). Capecitabine is a modest-benefit standard, not a proven cure-maker.

Resection outcomes depend heavily on margins and early recurrence. Five-year OS is 28.7% after R0, 13.9% after R1 and 0% after R2 resection. It reaches 49.8% in patients without very early recurrence vs 8.9% with it, and 73.4% of patients in one 933-patient series recurred ([O'Bryan, Clin Adv Hematol Oncol 2023](https://www.hematologyandoncology.net/files/2023/11/ho1123_OBryan_He_feature_v2.pdf)).

The surprising success case is transplant for **very early iCCA** (a single tumour of 2 cm or less in a cirrhotic liver). In that group, 5-year OS was **65%** with 18% recurrence, vs 45% OS and 61% recurrence for advanced iCCA ([Healio, Sapisochin 2016](https://www.healio.com/news/hepatology/20160811/cirrhotic-patients-with-early-icca-possible-candidates-for-lt)). The series had only 15 patients, so HepatoScope should present transplant for iCCA as protocol-based and centre-specific.

## Hepatoblastoma is the field's clearest cure story

Hepatoblastoma usually occurs before age 3. Its staging is PRETEXT (I–IV), which counts tumour-involved liver sections and adds annotation factors V, P, E, F, R, C, N and M. AFP response guides treatment, and the aim is complete resection, either upfront, after chemotherapy or by transplant ([NCI PDQ](https://www.cancer.gov/types/liver/hp/child-liver-treatment-pdq)). Population survival has climbed from **36% (1973–82) to 81.9% five-year OS** in SEER patients diagnosed 2004–2011. Five-year OS is 89.8% after resection, 92.5% after transplant and 35.8% without surgery ([Feng, Ann Transl Med](https://atm.amegroups.org/article/view/30387/html)).

Trial outcomes by risk group are consistently high. In **SIOPEL-3**, standard-risk cisplatin alone gave 3-year EFS/OS of 83%/95% vs 85%/93% with PLADO, so the less intensive regimen worked as well. **SIOPEL-4** (high risk) gave 3-year EFS 76% and OS 83%, including 77%/79% for children with metastases. **COG AHEP0731** reported 5-year EFS of 87% (low), 88% (intermediate) and 55% (high risk), falling to 29% in high-risk children with small-cell undifferentiated histology ([NCI PDQ](https://www.cancer.gov/types/liver/hp/child-liver-treatment-pdq)). The **CHIC-HS** four-group risk model (very low, low, intermediate, high) was validated in 2025 on 403 patients. EFS was higher than in the original dataset for intermediate and high risk, which the authors credit to improved therapy ([EJC Paediatric Oncology via DOAJ](https://doaj.org/article/ee09da668f204d3b8900290732495200)). For unresectable disease, transplant outcomes include 76% five-year survival (UNOS 1987–2012), 81% three-year EFS (SPLIT 2011–2019) and 87.4% five-year OS after living-donor transplant ([NCI PDQ](https://www.cancer.gov/types/liver/hp/child-liver-treatment-pdq)).

The quality-of-survival breakthrough is **sodium thiosulfate**, which protects hearing during cisplatin. In SIOPEL-6, hearing loss was 33% vs 63% (RR 0.52) with no survival penalty: 3-year OS was 98% vs 92% ([Brock NEJM 2018, PMC6117111](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6117111)). The FDA label analysis of the same trial reports **39% vs 68% (RR 0.58)** in 114 patients. It reports a smaller, non-significant reduction in the localized subset of COG ACCL0431 (44% vs 58%). The FDA approved Pedmark on **Sep 20, 2022** ([FDA](https://www.fda.gov/drugs/resources-information-approved-drugs/fda-approves-sodium-thiosulfate-reduce-risk-ototoxicity-associated-cisplatin-pediatric-patients)), and the EU authorised Pedmarqsi on **May 26, 2023** ([EMA](https://www.ema.europa.eu/en/medicines/human/EPAR/pedmarqsi)). The two SIOPEL-6 numbers come from different analyses, so the app should cite the one it shows. CTNNB1 variants occur in **more than 80%** of cases, but no targeted therapy exists ([NCI PDQ](https://www.cancer.gov/types/liver/hp/child-liver-treatment-pdq)). PHITT (NCT03017326) is the overarching international trial, and its primary results were not found as of November 2026 ([NCI](https://www.cancer.gov/clinicaltrials/NCI-2019-02843)).

## Real-world survivors mirror trials for fit patients, but survival depends on stage at diagnosis

At population level, the largest gap is stage at diagnosis. SEER reports 5-year relative survival for liver and intrahepatic bile duct cancer of **21.9% overall: 37.4% localized, 13.4% regional and 3.6% distant**. Forty-five percent of cases are now diagnosed while localized. The estimates for 2026 are 42,340 new US cases and 30,980 deaths ([SEER](https://seer.cancer.gov/statfacts/html/livibd.html)). Blue Faery publishes different stage figures (45%/26%/18%) with no per-figure attribution ([Blue Faery](https://www.bluefaery.org/statistics)). HepatoScope should use SEER. On prevention, universal HBV vaccination in Taiwan cut childhood liver-cancer mortality by about **70% in boys** (rate ratio 0.30) ([CEBP 2003](https://aacrjournals.org/cebp/article/12/1/57/115009/Trends-in-the-Incidence-of-Hepatocellular)).

Trial benefit appears to carry into practice for Child-Pugh A patients. The prospective French **CHIEF** cohort (552 patients on first-line atezolizumab–bevacizumab, 81% Child-Pugh A) reported **1-year OS of 66%**, ORR 27% and median PFS 4.6 months, which the investigators called "similar to IMbrave150" ([FMC-HGE](https://www.fmcgastro.org/?p=33226)). Its peer-reviewed median OS was not verified. Depth of response tracks with survival. In IMbrave150, any tumour shrinkage at 6 months was associated with median OS of 24.9 vs 14.0 months ([Karger, Liver Cancer 2025](https://karger.com/lic/article/14/5/539/922666/Depth-and-Duration-of-Response-Are-Associated-with)). In HIMALAYA, 5-year OS reached **50.7%** among STRIDE patients with more than 25% shrinkage ([Targeted Oncology](https://www.targetedonc.com/view/stride-regimen-shows-sustained-5-year-os-benefit-in-unresectable-hcc)). Both are exploratory landmark analyses that are prone to guarantee-time bias. They describe associations and do not show that shrinkage causes longer survival.

Complete responses are the closest thing to "remission stories" in advanced HCC, and they are uncommon. In a 28-centre series of 3,933 ICI-treated patients, **174 (about 4%) achieved an mRECIST CR**. Their **3-year OS was 86%**. Among those who stopped treatment during an ongoing CR, 3-year RFS was 81% if they had continued at least 6 months past CR, vs 55% with a shorter duration. A smaller series of 7 patients who electively stopped after CR found 1 recurrence. The reviewers conclude there is **no validated stopping rule** and that cessation should be an individualized discussion ([He & Wang 2026, PMC13660773](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13660773)).

Downstaging is the other real-world success. In the UCSF/Region 5 protocol, **65.3% of 118 patients** were successfully downstaged. Their post-transplant 5-year survival was **77.8%**, against **81%** for within-Milan controls (p=0.69). Intention-to-treat survival was lower, at 56.1% vs 63.3% ([UCSF, Hepatology 2015](https://escholarship.org/content/qt7rk815cn/qt7rk815cn.pdf?t=rt6jzr)). Systemic conversion with PD-1 inhibitors plus TKIs is earlier-stage evidence. One single-centre, mostly HBV cohort of 63 patients reported 60.3% conversion, 21 resections and pathological complete response in 11.1% of all patients, with no control group ([JGO 2026](https://jgo.amegroups.org/article/view/116893/html)). In iCCA, the durable-responder evidence comes from FIGHT-202: 30% of pemigatinib responders kept their response for at least 12 months, and responders had median OS of 46.1 vs 13.7 months, again a guarantee-time-biased comparison ([PMC11190465](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11190465)). The verified peer-support source is **Blue Faery**, which offers one-on-one peer support and a survivor blog ([Blue Faery](https://www.bluefaery.org/one-on-one-support)). Individual case reports should carry an "individual story, not typical" label.

## Six personas trace the journey from surveillance to second-line decisions

The adult HCC journey runs through surveillance, imaging and diagnosis, BCLC staging, tumour board, treatment and follow-up. The AGA's April 2026 update confirms that **ultrasound plus AFP every 6 months** remains the preferred surveillance for cirrhosis of any cause. For chronic HBV without cirrhosis, surveillance applies to a risk-scored subset (PAGE-B, REAL-B). It is not recommended for non-cirrhotic MASLD or after hepatitis C cure without cirrhosis. GALAD and abbreviated MRI remain investigational ([AGA/GI & Hepatology News](https://news.gastro.org/issues/2026/04/clinical-practice-update-ultrasound-plus-afp-remains-the-standard-for-hcc-surveillance/)).

BCLC then maps each stage to treatment. Stage 0 goes to resection or ablation. Stage A goes to resection, ablation or, with portal hypertension or multifocal disease within Milan, transplant. Stage B splits three ways: extended-criteria transplant, TACE, or systemic therapy for diffuse disease. Stage C goes to atezolizumab–bevacizumab, which requires Child-Pugh A and no high-risk varices, or to STRIDE or a TKI. Stage D goes to palliative care. "Treatment stage migration" allows a patient to move to a later-stage option when the stage-matched one is unsuitable ([BCLC 2022](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8866082/)).

Caregiver research is thin. In a 16-person ASCO 2024 study, the main burdens were logistics (appointments and transport) and symptom support, and views of palliative care were a major theme ([Physician's Weekly](https://www.physiciansweekly.com/post/understanding-the-needs-of-patients-with-hepatocellular-carcinoma-their-caregivers)).

The personas below are **illustrative composites** built on that epidemiology. HCC is male-predominant, HBV dominates in Asian and African populations, and HCV and alcohol dominate in the US ([Blue Faery statistics](https://www.bluefaery.org/statistics)). iCCA carries FGFR2 fusions in up to 15% and IDH1 mutations in up to 20% ([ESMO](https://oncologypro.esmo.org/education-library/factsheets-on-biomarkers/fgfr2-fusions-testing-in-intrahepatic-cholangiocarcinoma)). Hepatoblastoma is linked to very low birth weight and to Beckwith-Wiedemann syndrome and FAP ([NCI PDQ](https://www.cancer.gov/types/liver/hp/child-liver-treatment-pdq)). The personas are not real patients.

## Clinician mode needs provenance-stamped answer cards; patient mode needs natural frequencies

Clinician use cases cluster around tumour-board decisions:

- choosing between atezolizumab–bevacizumab and STRIDE when variceal bleeding risk is present
- using ramucirumab at AFP of 400 ng/mL or higher
- sequencing FGFR inhibitors after progression on GemCis plus immunotherapy, noting infigratinib's 2024 US withdrawal ([ASCO Post](https://ascopost.com/news/may-2024/fda-withdraws-approval-of-cholangiocarcinoma-drug-indication-for-fgfr-inhibitor/))
- ordering NGS for iCCA
- interpreting variants of uncertain significance
- matching patients to trials

The guideline logic for these questions (BCLC, ESMO/ESCAT) must be encoded separately from HepatoScope's research databases. Those databases are ClinicalTrials.gov, ChEMBL, Open Targets, ClinVar, gnomAD, UniProt, AlphaFold/PDB, GTEx, Reactome, STRING, Europe PMC and openFDA FAERS. FAERS must be labelled as spontaneous reports that cannot establish incidence or causality.

The Five Rights of clinical decision support translate directly into product rules: the right information, to the right person, in the right format, through the right channel, at the right point in the workflow. Lower-severity guidance should be passive rather than interruptive, because poorly targeted pop-up alerts are overridden **49–96%** of the time ([CASRAI summary](https://casrai.org/guides/5-rights-of-cds)). Each card should show the following:

- regulatory status: regular, accelerated, withdrawn, or "not approved, CRL"
- the analysis cut (label-interim vs updated)
- primary vs crossover-adjusted HRs
- the source and retrieval date

The status flag matters most for drugs whose standing changes. Pemigatinib, zanidatamab, CARES-310 and pembrolizumab in second-line HCC all need it.

Patient mode should target reading level and numeracy. NIH recommends patient materials at a **sixth-grade reading level at most**, yet American Cancer Society online materials average grade 10. LLM rewriting brought grade levels down to 5.6–7.6 with 0–3.3% inaccuracy, which the authors judged not yet reliable without human review ([JMIR 2025](https://www.jmir.org/2025/1/e69955)). The CDC Clear Communication Index scores 20 items, including numbers and risk, and sets a **passing score of 90 or more out of 100** ([CDC CCI](https://www.cdc.gov/ccindex/tool/faq.html)).

From these sources, HepatoScope's patient rules follow:

- Express outcomes as natural frequencies with a fixed denominator, for example "about 20 out of 100 people" in place of "HR 0.76." Use 100-person icon arrays, and always pair a success figure with its complement.
- Rephrase a median as "about half of people lived longer than…"
- Label each number's population: trial vs real world, stage, and liver function.
- Make prognosis opt-in through progressive disclosure.
- Never call a PFS gain "living longer."
- Label case reports as individual stories.
- Keep a separate hepatoblastoma track for parents.
- Put an "education, not medical advice" banner and a "questions to ask your care team" checklist on every page.

WCAG 2.2 AA accessibility and NCI dictionary wording for the glossary are the right standards to apply, but neither was fetched in this research. Check both before release.

## Conclusion

Measured by cure, liver cancer's success stories come from catching disease early or treating children. Transplant within Milan criteria, downstaging that reaches transplant, very-early iCCA transplant and risk-adapted hepatoblastoma therapy all produce survival in the 65–95% range. Systemic therapy in adults has instead shifted the tail of the survival curve. It turns a 1-in-10 five-year survival into roughly 1 in 5, and it produces a rare but durable complete-response group whose 3-year survival approaches that of curable disease. The 2024–2026 trial wave adds a pattern HepatoScope should build in from the start: **PFS gains repeatedly failed to become survival gains**. That happened adjuvantly (IMbrave050), with TACE combinations (EMERALD-1, LEAP-012) and with first-line targeted therapy (FIGHT-302). The app's evidence model should therefore treat OS, not PFS or response rate, as the threshold for calling a treatment a "success." It should also show regulatory status and data cut as first-class, dated fields.

For product design, the main risk is miscalibration rather than missing data. Showing patients cross-trial medians, responder-only survival or unlabelled case reports would overstate what treatment usually achieves. A trustworthy two-mode HepatoScope needs three things. Every number should be tied to a stage, a population and a verification status. Patients should see natural frequencies with their complements. Clinicians should see status-flagged answer cards that are re-checked whenever an accelerated approval converts, a CRL resolves (as with CARES-310) or a confirmatory trial reads out (as with HERIZON-BTC-302). The unverified-items table works as a release checklist: nothing in it should reach users until someone has checked it against the primary source.
